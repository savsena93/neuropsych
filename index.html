Informed consent

Participant code: Not assigned

Purpose. You are being invited to take part in a PSYC 728 demonstration of an electronic neuropsychological assessment toolkit developed at the University of Health and Allied Sciences. This is an educational feasibility project used to test how structured cognitive screening and symptom questionnaires are administered on a tablet. It is not a medical diagnosis and is not intended to replace a clinical evaluation by a qualified clinician.

What will happen. The examiner may assign one or more of the following assessments: the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), the Revised Quick Cognitive Screening Test (RQCST), the Rey Complex Figure Test, the Trail-Making Test (TMT), and the Brief Symptom Inventory-18 (BSI-18). These tasks may include answering questions, following spoken directions, selecting responses, writing or speaking brief answers, and using a finger or stylus to draw or trace figures. Some activities are timed, and some instructions may be read aloud by the examiner.

How the tasks are used. MoCA includes brief memory, language, visual-spatial, attention, and orientation tasks. RQCST covers orientation, verbal reasoning, visual-spatial tasks, and memory items. The Rey Complex Figure involves copying a figure, followed by immediate and delayed recall drawing tasks. Trail-Making assesses visual attention and task switching by connecting numbers and letters in sequence. BSI-18 is a brief self-report questionnaire about common psychological symptoms over the past seven days. The examiner may score the performance as part of the educational assessment process.

Time and records. The duration depends on the number of tests assigned and how long you take to complete them. Your responses, drawings, completion times, and the examiner's scoring notes will be stored under your participant code and may be reviewed by the examiner and administrator. The results may be compiled into a combined assessment report for course evaluation.

Voluntary participation. Taking part is voluntary. You may decline any activity, ask questions, pause, or stop at any time without penalty. You may withdraw your consent and request erasure of your participant record and associated assessment data, subject to the local record-management procedures used in this prototype.

Privacy and limits. The application uses a participant code rather than your name. The data are kept for this educational feasibility project and are not intended to be used alone to make health, employment, academic, or treatment decisions. Data may be stored on the assessment device or in the course demonstration deployment configured for this project.
